# Persona review 20: the curious kid, first run

Ran on version 1.26.1 (build `38df0ad`) on 2026-11-07, on the published site. Profile and brief: [42](../../issues/42-reviews.md), profile 20. A child of about ten, touch only, no MIDI, twenty minutes. Mostly an iPad (810 × 1080, Playwright WebKit for what it looks like, Playwright Chromium with real touch events for the drags), then an iPhone (390 × 664, WebKit) for the first minute and the whole Demo.

**Not checked:** the sound. The browsers ran headless, so nothing was heard; where a card or a Preset says what to hear, only what the screen shows was checked. Playwright's WebKit leaves a mouse pointer where a tap was, which opened one hover tip (the Amp Scope's) after the welcome closed; a real tablet would not, so it is not counted. The screenshots and scripts stayed in the session's scratch folder and are not kept.

## First impression

"There's a big green button and an orange one. I pressed the green one and the whole thing started moving by itself! Wiggly lines everywhere, knobs turning on their own, and a little blue water drop walking on the boxes. I don't know what an Oscillator is, but I wanted to touch everything."

## What they did, minute by minute

1. **0:00, the welcome.** Big logo, a little drop next to the name, lots of small writing (Made by, License, Privacy) that they skipped. "Touch and hold any control to see what it does" was the one line they read. Tapped **Play the Demo**, the green one.
2. **0:10 to 3:00, the Demo.** Stayed for all of it, about three minutes. The knobs turn by themselves, the page scrolls to follow them, the piano lights up. On the phone, Bead surfed the LFO's wavy line during Tremolo Keys: the best moment of the Demo. The card's words were half for them: "a deep bass going 'wah-wah'" and "plucked like strings" yes; "Osc 2 on the Note, a few cents off" and "a sine swung by another 3.56 times higher" no.
3. **3:00, the piano.** Bashed out C, E, G. "Tap a key to start the sound" went away at the first tap. The piano was two octaves low (C2) after the Demo was left halfway, and the Patch said "Last session", which meant nothing to them.
4. **4:00, Bead.** Tapped it: a spray of droplets. Pressed on it and dragged: it dangled, flew across the screen when let go, bounced and landed on another box with a splash. They wanted to do it again and again. No one told them it could be thrown; they found it because kids drag things.
5. **5:00, the knobs.** Turned Cutoff all the way down with a finger: Bead turned into an ice cube, shivering. Turned it up: it melted. Turned Resonance all the way up: Bead screamed, eyes wide, tail straight. They laughed at both and went looking for more knobs that do something to Bead.
6. **7:00, touch and hold.** Held Cutoff, Resonance, Octave, FM, Volume, Attack, Depth, Rate, the Waveform buttons, Demo, Song, Hold and Tutorial. A tip came every time, near the finger. Cutoff's ("turn it down for a darker, muffled sound, up for a brighter one") and Attack's ("short for a sharp start (a pluck), long for a slow swell") they understood. FM's is about 200 words and they stopped after the first line. The Demo's tip lists fifteen kinds of music in one breath.
7. **9:00, the Presets.** Opened the name at the top: a wall of buttons. Skipped the grown-up rows and went straight for the names that sound like things: Laser, Helicopter, Siren, Robot, Toy Piano, 808 Boom, Cowbell, Ocean. Each one shows a line when loaded, and the ends of those lines are the fun part: "rotor blades", "a siren's wail", "like a robot talking", "the metal tines of a toy", "like waves on a beach". The beginnings ("A fast square LFO chopping the Filter on Noise and a Sub") they skipped.
8. **12:00, the Tour.** Six cards, four paragraphs each on the first four. They read the first line of each ("Watch the wave change shape") and watched: the wave did change, the Filter's lines did sink, the Amp's picture froze. The rest of each card was too much reading. Bead stood next to the ringed control on two cards, pointing.
9. **15:00, a Tutorial.** Pressed "Build a Sub Ladder" at the end of the Tour. The Steps are short ("Make Osc 1 a Triangle.") and "Do it for me" does it. The notes after each Step were the best words of the day: a sine "like a whistle or a flute", a triangle "like a recorder" (they play one at school), a square "hollow, like a clarinet". "A ninth as strong" and "an oboe" lost them. At the end: "This is the Sub Ladder Preset, built by you." They liked that.
10. **18:00, About.** Found the little drop next to the name, tapped it, and got Bead's card: the glass of water and the burp, the umbrella, the water gun, the fishing. "To send it away... play B, E, A, D." They went back to the piano and could not find B: only the C keys have letters. (The reviewer then spelled B, E, A, D and D, E, A, D on the on-screen piano by touch: Bead melted away and came back, and played dead. Both work; a ten-year-old just cannot find the keys.)
11. **19:00, the Song.** Pressed Song. It played on the last sound they had, a soft noise, so it was a "shhh" in rhythm, and Bead danced to it. Time was up.

## What worked

- **The first minute.** The green button is the obvious first tap, the Demo starts at once and moves on its own, and a kid stays.
- **Bead.** The ice cube, the scream, the throw, the splash, the droplets, the surf on the LFO's line. Every knob they touched did something to it. This is what kept them.
- **Touch and hold.** Works on every control they tried, and the first sentence of most tips is plain.
- **The Presets with real-world names** and the picture at the end of each line.
- **The Tutorial's notes**, comparing each wave to an instrument a kid knows, and "built by you".

## Where they got stuck or bored

- **B, E, A, D.** The keys have no letters except C, so Bead's one spelled trick a kid is told about cannot be played (finding 1).
- **The long texts.** The Tour's cards, the FM and Demo tips, the Demo's captions and the start of each Preset's line are written for grown-ups: they read the first line and stopped (finding 2).
- **Bead's card** talks about the mouse, which a tablet has none of: tickling does not work with a finger at all. And it never says Bead can be picked up and thrown, the best trick on a tablet (finding 3).
- **The Presets list** is long, and its first rows (Shapes, Intervals, Filter, Envelope) are lessons, not toys; the toys (Drums, Move & FX) are at the very bottom.

## Words they did not understand

Oscillator and Osc (the panel says "OSC 1"), Harmonics, Spectrum, Scope, Cutoff, Resonance, low-pass, band-pass, Envelope, Attack, Decay, Sustain, Release, LFO, Depth, Destination, FM, Ring, Sub, Noise, Mixer, Patch, Preset, Init, Hz and kHz, dB, cents, fundamental, partials, Cycle Lock, tremolo, vibrato, oboe, "a ninth as strong".

Many of these are explained where they first come (the Tour defines Oscillator, Harmonics, Filter, Cutoff, Envelope's four stages and LFO), so a kid who reads the whole card gets them. A kid who reads one line does not.

## The "why?"s

- **Answered:** why a saw buzzes and a sine sounds pure (the Harmonics, in the Tour and the Tutorial); why turning the Cutoff down makes it darker (the high ones are taken away); what an LFO does ("a slow, invisible hand on a knob").
- **Left open:** why it is called an Envelope (a kid knows envelopes as what letters go in); what L, F and O stand for (the site never spells out "low-frequency"); why Bead freezes when the Cutoff goes down (it is funny, but nothing says the sound has gone "cold" and muffled too); why the Song sounded like "shhh" (it plays on whatever sound is loaded, which the Song's tip says, but a kid does not hold Song before pressing it).

## Did they learn one thing?

Yes: "Every sound is a stack of little sounds, and the shape of the wave decides which ones are in it. A sine has just one, so it's like a whistle." That came from the Tutorial's notes, not from the Tour.

## Would they

- **Like it:** yes. Bead and the Demo; "the drop turned into an ice cube!"
- **Share it:** yes, with a friend, to show them Bead screaming and the Helicopter.
- **Come back:** yes, to find more things Bead does. They know there are more ("All its secrets") but that link goes to a page for grown-ups on GitHub.
- **Recommend it:** yes to other kids "who like buttons", but they'd say "don't read, just press things".

## Three findings, ranked

1. **Bead's B, E, A, D cannot be played by someone who does not know the piano:** the on-screen keys carry no letters but C, so a kid told by Bead's card to "play B, E, A, D" cannot find B. Letters on the keys (or on the keys of the four Notes, while Bead's card is open) would make the trick theirs.
2. **The words stop a ten-year-old after the first line:** the Tour's cards (four paragraphs), the FM tip (about 200 words), the Demo's tip (fifteen kinds of music in one sentence), the Demo's captions ("a few cents off", "3.56 times higher") and each Preset's line before its picture. The first lines and the Tutorial's instrument comparisons work; the rest is for grown-ups.
3. **Bead's card in About is written for a mouse:** "giggles when you tickle it with the mouse" (on a tablet a finger cannot tickle it at all), and it leaves out picking it up and throwing it, the trick a tablet does best, which a kid found only by dragging.

## For the inbox, one line each

- Review 20 (curious kid, 1.26.1): the on-screen piano names only the Cs, so a kid told by Bead's card to play B, E, A, D cannot find the keys; letters on the keys would make Bead's word tricks playable without knowing the piano.
- Review 20: the Tour's cards, the FM and Demo tips, the Demo's captions and the start of each Preset's line lose a ten-year-old after one line ("a few cents off", "3.56 times higher", "partials"); the first lines and the Tutorial's instrument comparisons ("like a recorder") are what they keep.
- Review 20: Bead's card in About says to tickle it "with the mouse", which a finger cannot do, and never says it can be picked up and thrown, the best trick on a tablet.

## Aside, outside the kid's view

In Chromium only, sending Bead away with B, E, A, D logs `TypeError: Cannot read properties of null (reading 'offsetParent')` about a second later, from the page reader's `holder().offsetParent` (it seems to run once more after Bead has gone). Nothing visible breaks: B, E, A, D again brings Bead back. WebKit logs nothing.
